Geo Digest · 2026-W40 · Sep 28–Oct 4, 2026

Nobody can win the Iran war in the open, so it moved into spies, distant blockades and the price of diesel and money.

Britain suspects Iran behind a plot at an air base. America adds a carrier while short of weapons. Fuel and borrowing costs climb from Kyiv to Hanoi.

First, the rule that never changes

The rule

Q: What happens to a war that neither side can win in the open?

A: It does not stop. It moves to where fighting is cheap and hard to answer: spies, blockades from far away, and the other side's daily costs.

Q: Who pays for a fight like that?

A: Mostly people far from it. They pay through the price of fuel and the price of loans.

Think of it like this. Two shops on one street cannot beat each other on price. So one blocks the other's delivery road, and the other sends people to break its windows at night. Every customer on the street now pays more. This week the street is the world's fuel and money.

The whole week as one picture

  1. Why does diesel cost more this week?Because the world is short of ready-made fuel, not of crude oil. Ukraine's drones keep hitting Russian refineries, so Russia now buys diesel instead of selling it, and far fewer tankers pass the Strait of Hormuz. In Britain, diesel passed £2 a litre for the first time.
  2. Why does the war that closed the strait still go on?Because neither side can win it in the open, and neither can afford to lose. Foreign Affairs calls it a new forever war. America adds an aircraft carrier but admits some weapons run low. Iran cannot beat a navy, so it looks for other ways to hurt.
  3. What does Iran do against a stronger navy?It fights where ships cannot help. Britain says Iran was likely involved in an incident at RAF Fairford, an English air base the US uses. A terror researcher sees signs of Iran behind an axe attack on a Dubai airliner's cockpit. The war is moving onto allied soil.
  4. Who pays for a war like this, besides soldiers?Everyone who borrows. Lenders fear that costly fuel keeps prices rising, so they ask for more interest. Hồ Quốc Tuấn shows that oil and US borrowing costs now rise and fall together more closely than at any time since 2000. A firm can survive months of costly fuel, not years of costly loans.
  5. So what reaches Vietnam and Switzerland?Two very different bills. Vietnam pays twice, in fuel and in money, just as it needs its fastest quarter in years to hit its growth target. Switzerland barely pays yet: prices rose only a little, and its voters chose to keep working with neighbours rather than stand apart.
  6. What can change the answer?Refined fuel, not speeches. If Washington signs a diesel export ban, Europe goes into winter short and the bill grows. If diesel prices fall for several weeks in a row while the war goes on, the cheap fight is losing its bite. Watch the pump price.

The weakest link, and what the chain shows. The arrow from China's petrol export halt to Vietnam's fuel bill is the weak one: Geopolitical Futures reports the halt, but no source says Vietnam's supply was cut, only Zeihan's earlier finding that Vietnam buys refined fuel from China. Vietnam's October fuel-import figures will test it. The chain shows that three separate wars and one refiner meet in one tank of diesel.

Now, step by step

Vietnam

The mechanism

Q: What does a fast-growing country run on?

A: Cheap fuel and cheap money. Vietnam controls the price of neither.

Q: If both cost more at the same time, what happens?

A: The government must choose between its growth target and the losses of the people who do the building.

Think of it like this. A young builder signs many contracts at fixed prices. Then diesel doubles and the bank raises the rate on his loan. The more houses he finishes, the more money he loses. But his boss wants even more houses this winter.

This week the builder is Vietnam's building firms. The boss is the government's growth target for 2026.

  1. Vietnam's economy grew 9.95% in the third quarter compared with a year earlier. That is its fastest pace in four years.becausethe state pushes growth hard, mainly through building and exports. A strong quarter is the base that this year's plan rests on. Nikkei Asia, Oct 3
  2. But even that is not enough. To reach the government's double-digit growth target for 2026, the last quarter must grow more than 12.5% while inflation stays under 4.5%.becauseNikkei puts the need for the last quarter at about 12%, Hồ Quốc Tuấn at more than 12.5%. Hồ Quốc Tuấn (Đọc Chậm), Oct 3
  3. So why does the government need a record quarter, and why now? Because the target covers all of 2026 and three quarters are gone, so the missing growth must come in the last one.becausethe government set a double-digit target for the whole year. No source this week reports why it chose that number, only that the last quarter must now carry it. Hồ Quốc Tuấn (Đọc Chậm), Oct 3
  4. But that quarter must be built with diesel, and diesel got much more expensive. In Vietnam it went from about 19,000 dong a litre in early February to over 44,780 in early April. In early September it was still about 30,100.becausethe Iran war and Ukraine's strikes on Russian refineries both took ready-made fuel off the world market. Vietnam imports much of its fuel. Hồ Quốc Tuấn (Đọc Chậm), Oct 3
  5. So builders on fixed-price contracts lose money. Tuổi Trẻ reports that 20 large building firms in Quảng Ngãi province "kêu cứu" (cry for help): "Càng làm càng lỗ" (the more we work, the more we lose).becausewhen diesel moved, their contract prices did not. Hồ Quốc Tuấn (Đọc Chậm), Oct 3
  6. And their fuel can get scarcer still. Vietnam buys refined fuel from China, and Chinese refiners suspended petrol exports on 1 October, about a week after the Trump-Xi summit.becausePeter Zeihan found that dependence on China earlier. No source this week says Vietnam's own supply was cut, so this step rests on that older finding. Geopolitical Futures, Oct 2
What most people missFast growth is not the same as jobs for the young. Vietnam grew 8.0% in 2025, and its income per person more than doubled since 2010. Yet youth unemployment rose 2.8 percentage points over the same years East Asia Forum, Oct 2. A push for a record quarter through building lifts the total. It does not by itself give a young graduate a job.

Switzerland

The mechanism

Q: Does a small country stay safe by standing apart?

A: No. It stays safe by being steady and useful to its neighbours, and by keeping its own money sound.

Q: So what is a vote on neutrality really about?

A: Whether to write down now, for good, that you will never take a side, before anyone asks you to.

Think of it like this. A quiet family lives on a street that is getting rougher. One cousin wants a higher wall and a written promise never to help anyone. The others want good terms with the neighbours and money in the bank. This week the family voted.

This week the family is Switzerland. The cousin with the wall is the SVP, the largest party. The neighbours are the EU.

  1. On 27 September Swiss voters clearly rejected the . The SVP, the largest party, had proposed to write permanent armed neutrality into the constitution, ban alliances and limit sanctions.becauseNZZ: «Die Vorlage wurde klar verworfen» (the proposal was clearly rejected). NZZ, Sep 27
  2. So why say no, and why now? Europe's protection looks less certain this year. Voters said no and so kept their room to work with others. The SVP's own reasons for the initiative are not reported in this week's sources.becausea rule in the constitution binds every future government, so the vote was about every future crisis, not only this one. NZZ, Sep 27
  3. So the next question is Europe. The upper house of parliament, the Council of States, approved the package of new treaties with the EU, including deals on health and food safety.becausea country that will not stand apart has to set its terms with its biggest market. The SVP calls the planned June 2027 referendum date election tactics. NZZ, Oct 1
  4. But the terms are not settled. The same chamber voted that EU citizens must show an extract from their criminal record. The EU's ambassador criticised that rule and said the EU expects Switzerland to keep its promises.becausefree movement of people is the price of access to the EU market, and the upper house tried to make that price smaller. NZZ, Oct 2
  5. And that quarrel reaches into the government itself. Economy minister Guy Parmelin, from the SVP, is leaving the seven-member Federal Council after 11 years. He said «Das politische Klima ist härter geworden» (the political climate has grown harsher).becausehe leaves just as the EU package heads for a vote, and his party attacks that vote's timetable. NZZ, Oct 2
  6. But that quarrel stopped at the money, the other half of Swiss safety. The Swiss National Bank kept its rate at 0%.becauseoil lifted inflation only from 0.6% to 0.8%. The bank names the Middle East as the main source of uncertainty. SNB, Sep 30
What most people missSwitzerland's calm about fuel prices is a choice, not luck. With petrol and diesel near record highs, Germany cut fuel taxes and Switzerland did not. NZZ argues that the Swiss restraint pays off NZZ, Oct 2. A government that does not spend on cheaper fuel keeps its money for the next shock.

United States

The mechanism

Q: How does a strong country leave a war it cannot win?

A: Slowly. It keeps the cheapest tools and tries to get a deal in return for leaving.

Q: And who carries the cost while it does that?

A: Its own voters at the fuel pump, and its allies on their own soil.

Think of it like this. A landlord cannot get a tenant out. So he stops fighting at the door, turns off the water at the street, and starts packing. He hopes the tenant signs before he leaves.

This week the landlord is the United States. The tenant is Iran. The water at the street is Iranian oil.

  1. Trump told Time magazine that "some" American weapons stocks are running low. He said that heavier strikes on Iran are possible after the November elections.becausea long war uses weapons faster than factories make them. The Army's top civilian, Dan Driscoll, argues that America and its allies must build weapons together for this new kind of war. BBC, Oct 1
  2. So America adds ships far from shore rather than fighting closer. It is sending another aircraft carrier and nearly 10,000 troops to the Middle East. Phillips O'Brien says those carriers now run a "distance blockade" that stops Iranian oil from reaching China.becauseships far offshore are safer than bases within Iran's reach. NZZ, Oct 2 · Phillips P. O'Brien, Sep 30
  3. So why fight from a distance, and why now? Because the war already cost America heavily in bases, aircraft and weapons, and a blockade from far away uses fewer weapons than strikes. And now because talks with Tehran apparently failed.becauseO'Brien reads the blockade as Washington negotiating the best terms for its own withdrawal. Phillips P. O'Brien, Sep 30
  4. So it pulls back where it can. The last American and British troops left Iraq as the mission against the Islamic State group ended.becausethe withdrawal follows a deal from two years ago, but it fits the pattern. Saudi Arabia asked for help against the Houthi rebels in Yemen and got intelligence and little else, the Lowy Institute reports. BBC, Sep 30
  5. But leaving slowly does not make the war cheap at home, and voters feel the cost first at the fuel pump. Trump threatened to ban diesel exports to bring pump prices down before the midterm elections, and pressed Europe to release its own diesel reserves.becauseZeihan says a ban alone traps fuel on the Gulf Coast, far from the places that need it. BBC, Oct 2 · Zeihan, Oct 1
  6. And the cost goes past the pump into loans and jobs. American hiring slowed sharply and unemployment rose, a month before the midterm elections.becauseTuấn reports US home loans above 7% and firms that delay plants and hiring because of costs. Borrowing follows fuel, and hiring follows borrowing. BBC, Oct 2
What most people missThe clearest sign of America's weaker hold this week is not in the Gulf but in Ottawa. Prime Minister Mark Carney said that Canada's military runs plans for an unlikely American invasion BBC, Oct 3. When the closest ally plans for the protector as a threat, every other ally takes note.

China

The mechanism

Q: How does a rising power get more of what it wants without a war?

A: It takes steps too small for anyone to fight over, while its rival is busy somewhere else.

Q: And what holds it back?

A: Its own debts at home.

Think of it like this. A neighbour moves the garden fence a little every night while you deal with a flood in your basement. But his own roof leaks.

This week the neighbour is China. The flood is the Iran war. The fence is the sea around the Philippines and the land in Laos.

  1. Experts split on what the Trump-Xi summit settled. But the Lowy Institute says Xi left with the better story.becauseGeorge Friedman lists what the summit did settle: tariffs cut on about $30bn of imports, the trade truce extended to January 2027, and Taiwan unresolved. When a meeting settles little, the side that tells its story better gains. War on the Rocks, Sep 30 · Lowy Institute, Oct 2
  2. So Beijing used the calm to push at sea. Beijing and Manila had a temporary deal on supplying a Philippine outpost at Second Thomas Shoal, a disputed reef in the South China Sea. That deal now appears broken, and China's army then held an exercise there.becauseThe Diplomat reports that the exercise came after a confrontation at the shoal. The Diplomat, Sep 28
  3. And it opened a new front further north. China claimed "sovereign rights" over the waters around Batanes, the Philippines' northernmost province, next to the strait that leads to Taiwan. Manila rejected the claim.becausethose waters sit on the route to Taiwan. The Diplomat, Oct 2
  4. And it took the same kind of small step on land. China's army, the PLA, opened a supply and training centre north of Vientiane, the capital of Laos, on Vietnam's western side.becauseThe Diplomat calls it a counterpart to the Ream naval base in Cambodia. Vietnam now has Chinese military sites in two of its neighbours. The Diplomat, Oct 1
  5. So why press its neighbours, and why now? Because each step stays below the line where the Philippines' defence treaty with America applies. And now because America is busy with Iran, and the summit truce runs to January 2027.becauseLowy warns that Beijing can squeeze Taiwan with an "administrative quarantine", pressure that never fires a shot. Lowy Institute, Oct 2
  6. But all of that costs money, and the money at home is weak. A record 670-plus Chinese lenders closed last year, and local debt is still not shrinking.becauseTianlei Huang finds that Beijing's plan to swap local governments' old debt for new eased their cash needs but did not cut what they owe. FT, Oct 4 · East Asia Forum, Oct 4
What most people missChina's weakest point is chips, and America's strength there depends on friends. Zeihan says China's own 7-nanometre chips work at about one ninth of the efficiency, so China needs nine data centres for every American one. No country, America included, makes leading chips alone Zeihan, Sep 29. The allies this war strains are the same allies that keep China behind.

Seen from their own capital

  • Moscow wants a deal before its army runs out of ground to lose. O'Brien says it tries to make Ukrainian cities unliveable to force one in the first half of 2027 Phillips P. O'Brien, Oct 4.

What most people miss

The part most people miss

The Iran war's fuel problem is no longer in the strait. Gulf crude exports are nearly back to pre-war levels. American escorts, small shuttle tankers and pipelines that go around made that possible BBC, Oct 2. Yet diesel hit records. The cost moved into shipping, insurance and refining: the price of moving oil by tanker was at times above $30 a barrel. As Tuấn puts it, "Vẫn vận chuyển được không đồng nghĩa với không có thiệt hại" (still being able to ship does not mean there is no damage) Hồ Quốc Tuấn (Đọc Chậm), Oct 3. Watching tankers in the strait tells you less than watching refineries.

So what would change the answer

The one step: whether the shortage of ready-made fuel eases before winter, or Washington closes its own diesel to the world. Every other bill this week, from Britain's pumps to Vietnam's builders to the price of loans, runs through that one tank.

You will see it first in one of these three:

If you see thisThen expect
A signed US order banning diesel exportsFuel piles up on America's Gulf Coast, prices fall there and rise in Europe and Latin America. Europe's winter gets riskier.
China's petrol export halt still in place in NovemberVietnam's next fuel price adjustment rises. Check whether Vietnam's fuel imports from China fall.
Another plot on European soil linked to Iran by a governmentEurope becomes a direct party. Expect more guards at bases and airports, and more pressure on Europe to join strikes.
If it holdsthe shortage stays and America bans exports: Europe enters winter with its reserves drawn down, lenders keep asking for more interest, and fixed-price builders keep losing money.
If it reversesonly actions count: no ban signed, Russian refineries back at work, and diesel prices falling for several weeks while the war continues. Then the cheap fight no longer works.

Now you try

The move to copy this week. When the strong side cannot win openly, ask where the fight moved, and who pays there.

Three questions to ask of any story next week:

1. If this side cannot win head-on, what cheaper way of hurting the other side does it have left? 2. Who pays that cost in the end, and do they live far from the front? 3. If I am right, what will I actually see? A number, a date or a shipment. Never a statement.

Now try it on one we have not answered. Trump threatened to ban American diesel exports to bring pump prices down before the November midterm elections. Run the three questions on it. Does he sign a ban before election day, or does Europe's release of its own reserves let him avoid it? If he signs, does the British pump price go up or down in the month after?

Write your answer down before next Saturday. Next week's report checks it, and that is the only way to learn whether your reasoning was right or only comfortable.

The terms used above

Strait of Hormuz
The narrow sea passage at the mouth of the Gulf that a large share of the world's traded oil ships through. Its importance is geographic, not political: there is no quick alternative route for most of that volume.
Neutrality initiative
A Swiss popular vote proposal to write strict neutrality into the constitution — which would bind future governments rather than leaving neutrality a matter of policy. Launched from the political right, so left-wing groups backing it is a genuine change in its odds.
