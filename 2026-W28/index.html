Geo Digest · 2026-W28 · Jul 04–11, 2026

Power is a stock, not a mood — and stocks can be counted.

This week outsiders counted four of them: America's munitions, its allied trust and its social cohesion; Russia's refining capacity; China's export surplus. Three came back smaller than the label, one came back bigger, and both directions land on the same two places — the price of a barrel of oil, and the order book of a Southeast Asian factory.

First, the rule that never changes

Every commitment spends a stock, and a commitment can only be sustained while the stock is replaced at least as fast as it is spent.

That is the whole engine. Bombs, refineries, allied trust, public consent — each one is a quantity, and each has a replacement time that is a physical fact. A missile factory takes years to expand. A refinery takes months to repair. Trust, once a partner has watched you fight alone, takes an election cycle at best. Resolve does not shorten any of these. So when the spend rate exceeds the replacement rate, a ceiling appears — and it is set by arithmetic, not by decision. Nobody voted for it and nobody can announce it away. This is why a country can look strong on every aggregate number and still be out of options: the balance sheet is the stock, and the ceiling is the flow.

Vietnam

The mechanism

A country that grows by assembling other people's products is renting its growth. Rent can be raised or cancelled by the landlord, so the only durable move is to buy the building — and buying takes skills and money you have to spend before you need them.

  1. Vietnam had a good data week: the 2026 GDP consensus was lifted to 7.3% and the inflation forecast cut to 4.8%.becausethe imported-energy shock that pushed May CPI to +5.60% did not compound — oil stayed off the boil long enough for the squeeze to ease at the margin. Bloomberg, Jul 9
  2. Hanoi answered by raising the ambition rather than banking the relief — an explicit push toward a 10% growth target.becauseat 7.3% Vietnam stays middle-income for a generation. Only a double-digit run closes the gap inside one working life, so the target is a statement about time, not about this year. Vietnam Weekly, Jul 9
  3. But the current model cannot produce 10%, and that is the week's real question.becausegrowth today comes from assembly — the middle of someone else's supply chain — and assembly volume scales with foreign orders, not domestic decisions. Foreign Affairs asks whether the private sector can carry the next stage precisely because state enterprises and FDI assembly demonstrably cannot. Foreign Affairs, Jul 8
  4. So Hanoi is trying to buy the building: semiconductors as statecraft.becauseassembly is rented and chip capability is owned. The Diplomat frames the positioning as statecraft rather than industrial policy because the window is finite and Hanoi knows it. The Diplomat, Jul 10
  5. That climb needs a skilled first rung, and the first rung is narrowing.becauseHồ Quốc Tuấn's Đọc Chậm argues AI is "cướp bậc thang đầu tiên" ("stealing the first rung") — entry-level work thins while responsibility concentrates on fewer senior staff. Đọc Chậm, Jul 5 A country climbing the value chain needs exactly the on-ramp that is getting steeper. (This is a labour-market piece, not a geopolitics one — read it as the human-capital variable under the ambition, not as this week's geo evidence.)
  6. Meanwhile the rung below is under attack from the other direction.becauseChina's surplus is verifiably larger than reported, and overcapacity that size has to land somewhere — Fulcrum names Southeast Asia as the somewhere, with Vietnam the most exposed name on the list. Setser, CFR, Jul 7 / Fulcrum, Jul 8
  7. And the power that was supposed to hold the ring is busy and unreliable.becausethe US is fighting a war it cannot end and no ally joined it. Zeihan, Jul 7 A weaker guarantor means less pressure to pick a side, and also less cover if China presses in the South China Sea.
  8. So Vietnam builds a small sphere of its own instead of waiting for someone else's.becauseit manages Laos and Cambodia while publicly denying great-power-style interference — a small power practising what it fears from larger ones. The Diplomat, Jul 6 Hedging is not neutrality; it is active management of a dependence you cannot escape.
What most people missEveryone reads the 7.3% upgrade as this week's Vietnam story. It is the smallest number in the week — a forecast, revised quarterly, by economists. The important one is the identity buried in the China essay: Vietnam's growth ceiling and China's surplus floor are the same number, seen from opposite ends of the supply chain. A forecast revises. A surplus that has to be hidden to be survivable does not.

Switzerland

The mechanism

A neutral country's safety is not produced by its neutrality. It is produced by a stable neighbourhood and cheap imported energy, both of which someone else pays for. When the payers stop paying, neutrality does not become dangerous — it becomes expensive.

  1. Nothing about Switzerland itself was scanned this week, and that is the first finding.becausethe NZZ English index served a stale cached snapshot again and produced zero in-window Swiss items — the recurring hole flagged in the scan health table. That is a coverage bug in `sources.md`, not Swiss calm. This ladder therefore runs on European items plus standing mechanism, not on Swiss reporting — grade it weaker than the US, China and Russia ladders below. scan-log
  2. What was scanned is the neighbourhood, and the neighbourhood was marked down on the security side.becauseNATO cohesion is now named as an explicitly eroding variable rather than an assumption Foreign Affairs, Jul 6, and Germany is publicly reconsidering its cold-war air-raid shelters FT, Jul 11. Swiss security is European security minus a signature — the geography is identical.
  3. It was marked down on the energy side in the same week.becausethe IEA's Fatih Birol called Europe's slow electrification a "major mistake" for energy independence. FT, Jul 11 Europe is still buying its security of supply on a world market rather than producing it.
  4. And two independent wars are squeezing that world market at once.becauseUkraine has a third to 42% of Russian refining offline O'Brien, Jul 5, while Iranian action put tankers onto a risky Omani bypass around Hormuz FT, Jul 11. Neither is decisive alone; together they are one supply shock with two causes.
  5. Switzerland imports essentially all of that energy and prices it in someone else's currency.becausestanding mechanism, not this week's scan — a small open economy with no hydrocarbons pays the world price plus the currency move, and can vote on neither.
  6. So both inputs that make Swiss neutrality affordable got more expensive in the same seven days, and neither because of anything Switzerland did.becausethat is the rule at the top. Neutrality is bought, not declared, and this week the invoice went up on both lines while the CH scorecard sat unchanged at 2.5 for lack of anything to move it.
What most people missThe recurring Swiss coverage hole is the finding. Four weeks of carrying CH forward unchanged is not evidence of calm — it is evidence of an unfixed feed, so read 2.5 as stale, not stable. Second, from this week's China essay: Switzerland's flattering equal-weighted composite is partly a measurement artifact of the same small-open-economy accounting distortions Setser flags for Ireland. Two of the four numbers on the scorecard are more confident than the data behind them, and CH is one of them.

United States

The mechanism

A superpower's strength is a set of stocks — munitions, allied trust, public consent. Each is spent quickly and replaced slowly. Spend all three at once and a ceiling appears that nobody chose.

  1. The US started a war with Iran and cannot end it.becauseIran has agreed to not a single one of Trump's demands, so there is no basis for an ending — while CENTCOM struck 80+ Iranian targets after Iran hit three tankers in Hormuz, and Washington briefed that it was preparing for weeks of exchange. O'Brien, Jul 9 / Geopolitical Futures, Jul 8
  2. Fighting it spends the first stock: advanced munitions and airframes.becausethe campaign ran down advanced-weapon stockpiles, with reports of high Reaper-drone loss rates, and exposed problems "that will take years to correct". O'Brien, Jul 9 Production lines take years; a war spends them in weeks.
  3. So the escalation options narrow even while the war continues.becauseyou cannot credibly threaten what you cannot resupply. That ceiling is physical, so it does not move when the rhetoric does — which is why 80+ targets did not convert into a settlement anyone could call a win.
  4. The second stock is allied trust, and the same war is spending it.becausenobody came to help the US in Iran Zeihan, Jul 7, and transatlantic trust is now treated as an eroding variable rather than a given Foreign Affairs, Jul 6. A hegemon whose partners sit out its war has already lost the thing that made it a hegemon: the assumption of followership.
  5. The third stock is domestic consent, and this week it got a number.becauseDalio's own Country Power Index reads the US as weakening on all three ingredients of a durable country at once — "the American public education system is broken, the public healthcare system is broken, and the government … is broken." The top 10% buy their way out of the broken systems; the bottom 60% fall behind, and their "willingness to compromise is falling while their willingness to fight the other side is increasing." He calls it an "emerging great conflict." Ray Dalio, Jul 4
  6. Those are not three problems. They are one.becauseDalio's model says a durable country educates its children, runs a system most people benefit from, and stays out of ruinous wars — and the US failed all three in the same week. A country that cannot compromise at home cannot form or exit a coherent strategy abroad. External overstretch and internal fracture are the same lost capacity to make a hard collective decision.
  7. And the floor still holds, which is exactly what makes it hard to see.because and frontier technology — the two heaviest legs under a Dalio weighting — did not move, and the aggregate numbers still look good. Late-stage decline from the inside is a strong balance sheet with a failing nervous system.
  8. So everyone else reads the same four measurements and presses.becausea hegemon publicly measured as unwilling to win and unable to cohere invites probes — China in the South China Sea, Russia in Ukraine, Iran in Hormuz. The measurement is not just a description of the week; it is an input into everyone else's next decision.
What most people missThe war looks like the news and the birthday essay looks like commentary. It is the other way round. The war is one week's spending; Dalio's read is the balance-sheet statement saying the spending cannot be sustained. And the hinge nobody says out loud: the US started this war. The decline here is not something happening to America — it is something America is doing, which is why no external event will stop it.

China

The mechanism

A country that sells far more than it buys is not accumulating strength. It is accumulating other people's willingness to keep buying — and that willingness is not a stock you own.

  1. China's customs goods surplus is about $1.2tn; its reported balance-of-payments current-account surplus is only $735bn.becausetwo anomalies open the gap — an inexplicable $125bn investment-income deficit on roughly $4tn of net foreign assets, and a 2022 SAFE methodology change with no credible public rationale. The gap it opened peaked near $260bn in 2023 and was still $184bn in 2024. Setser, CFR, Jul 7
  2. The comfortable explanation was customs fraud, and this week it was ruled out.becausethe mirror test. Every dollar China exports should show up as a partner's import, and partner data is not subject to Chinese accounting incentives. Across a nine-partner panel the mirror gap closed between 2018 and 2020 and stayed flat — while the customs-BOP gap exploded from 2021. Border fraud would have widened the mirror gap. It did not. Setser, CFR, Jul 7
  3. So whatever is shrinking the reported surplus lives inside SAFE's own construction of the balance of payments.becauseit is invisible to partner customs, and the only way to be invisible at the border is to happen after it. The true external surplus is meaningfully larger than $735bn and is being administratively made to disappear.
  4. A surplus you have to hide is a statement about how large it is.becausenobody smooths a flattering number. You smooth one that is politically radioactive — too big to defend to partners, too structural to shrink at home.
  5. It is structural because China produces far more than its own people consume.becauseevery unit of the gap must be sold to a foreigner or not sold at all, and fifteen years of declining to rebalance says the domestic side will not absorb it. The surplus is not a policy choice each year; it is the shape of the economy.
  6. Therefore the overcapacity has to land somewhere, and the somewhere is Southeast Asia.becauseFulcrum's "second China shock" is that same surplus arriving at the destination as cheap exports into SEA manufacturers. Fulcrum, Jul 8 This is the surplus and Vietnam's growth ceiling being the same number.
  7. And the surplus is being converted into leverage rather than into consumption.becauseThe Diplomat asks what follows rare earths as the next export-control weapon The Diplomat, Jul 7, and Lowy drops the benign-ambitions reading outright Lowy, Jul 8. Concentrated production is only leverage while nobody can replace you — which is precisely what an export control tests.
  8. So China ends the week stronger on trade and no closer to the one thing it lacks.becausereserve status runs on transparency and trust, and SAFE just demonstrated the opposite while state banks intervene to hold the currency down. The surplus of a hegemon with the monetary opacity of a mercantilist — which is why the scorecard holds CN at 2.2, not 2.8.
What most people missThe surplus reads as a weapon, and it is also a dependency. China needs foreigners to keep buying at that scale to keep its own people employed, so the leverage runs in both directions — but only one direction gets reported. The genuinely new thing this week is subtler: measurement itself became contested terrain. You cannot negotiate a rebalancing over numbers one party administratively controls. That is a more durable weapon than a tariff, because it makes the imbalance unarguable-about by making it unmeasurable-by-agreement.

What most people miss

The part most people miss

Nobody in this week's news is in Vietnam or Switzerland. The chain reaches both and never names either.

That is the habit worth training: the place a problem appears is not the place it was caused. Vietnamese transport inflation is set in Omsk and in the Strait of Hormuz. A thin order book in a Vietnamese factory is set by a SAFE accounting decision taken in Beijing in 2022. Swiss energy costs are set by Ukrainian drone range and by a European electrification choice made a decade ago. None of those causes is visible from inside the country that pays for them, and none of them will appear in that country's own news.

The second miss is about direction. Three of this week's four measurements came back short — America's munitions, America's allies, Russia's refining. One came back long: China's surplus is bigger than the label, not smaller. It is tempting to read the odd one out as good news for someone. It is not. A surplus that must be hidden to be survivable is the same kind of finding as an empty magazine — a quantity that cannot be sustained at its current rate, discovered by an outsider rather than announced by its owner.

So what would change the answer

Everything above hangs on one step: the war the United States cannot end.

It is upstream of almost every other node. It sets Hormuz risk, which is one of the two legs under the oil price. It spends the munitions, which sets the escalation ceiling. It spends the allied trust, which is what reaches Switzerland through European instability and reaches Vietnam as a weaker guarantor. End that war and half the oil shock, most of the alliance bleed and the probe incentive all unwind together. Watch that one thing instead of twenty.

What would reverse it: Iran handing Washington a face-saving formula. O'Brien's read is that Iran will not — which is why this hinge is stuck rather than merely tight.

The terms used above

Strait of Hormuz
The narrow sea passage at the mouth of the Gulf that a large share of the world's traded oil ships through. Its importance is geographic, not political: there is no quick alternative route for most of that volume.
China+1
The strategy of keeping factories in China but adding a second country — often Vietnam — to reduce tariff and political risk. Vietnam's export boom largely rides on it, which is why anything that questions Vietnam's independence from China (see **Transshipment**) threatens more than a single tariff line.
Reserve currency
A currency other countries hold as savings and use to settle trade — today mostly the dollar. It requires trust and openness above all, which is why a country can dominate trade and still not have one.
